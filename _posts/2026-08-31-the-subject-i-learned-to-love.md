I had a conversation with an intern on the team about education. We were talking about what we had studied, and naturally ended up discussing literature.

I did reasonably well in science and mathematics at school. I did considerably worse in English. That combination probably explains why I spent years thinking that English was a subject I did not like very much.

I see it differently now. The serious subject I studied in high school was English. History or philosophy might come second, but English was the one that stayed with me, even while I was busy thinking I had no use for it.

That is one of the privileges of education. You can spend years studying something before you are old enough to understand what it gave you. A poem, a novel, or a difficult essay may seem like an academic exercise at sixteen. Much later, you find yourself using the habits it taught you: paying attention to words, noticing what people leave unsaid, and trying to understand a mind very different from your own.

I am grateful that I was made to study a subject I thought I disliked. I only began to appreciate it many years later, when I finally had enough life behind me to read it properly.
