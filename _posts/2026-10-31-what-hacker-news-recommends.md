Karpathy's [HackerTube](https://hackertube.org/) collects 6,454 YouTube videos that Hacker News commenters have recommended to each other since 2007 and wraps them in a familiar interface, using the frame from the middle of each video instead of whatever thumbnail the uploader picked. Someone applied the same treatment to books: [HackerBooks](https://hackerbooks.org/) ranks 15,737 of them by how many different HN commenters recommended each one, with the comments sitting on the book page. Karpathy has a talent for ideas that are simple and, in hindsight, profound. Community recommendation systems have been good to me, though any of them can be gamed. I thought Goodreads was good, at least better than Amazon; its ratings sat closer to my own sense of good and bad. HackerBooks is very good because the recommendations come from Hacker News commenters with much less incentive to sell any particular book. LLMs are the right tool for this kind of recommendation, since the raw material is thousands of comments written for no purpose other than telling someone a book was worth their time.
