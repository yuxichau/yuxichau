Anthropic's usage policy update took effect on November 12, and the regional rules now spell out the workarounds people had been using. The [Supported Regions Policy](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/supported-regions) bans Claude for anyone physically located in an unsupported region, and it says outright that this includes staff of otherwise supported companies, so an employer in a supported country cannot buy seats for people sitting in restricted ones. The entity side was closed a year ago, when Anthropic [prohibited companies that are majority-owned](https://www.anthropic.com/news/updating-restrictions-of-sales-to-unsupported-regions), directly or indirectly, from unsupported regions regardless of where they operate. A VPN changes none of that, because the test is where you physically are. The enforcement was already visible before the new text arrived: I keep seeing people report banned accounts, mostly personal ones, [SCMP caught the Hong Kong end](https://www.scmp.com/tech/tech-war/article/3369526/hong-kong-users-caught-offguard-anthropic-tightens-vpn-access-claude) in early October, and Anthropic's own [transparency numbers](https://www.anthropic.com/transparency/system-trust-reporting) count 11.4 million banned accounts in the first half of 2026. That tally covers all policy violations rather than regional ones alone, but the anecdotes have real scale behind them.

Checking for a VPN is easier than most people assume. Open [cleanip.io's who-am-i page](https://cleanip.io/en-US/who-am-i) and count what a single visit hands over: exit IP and its reputation, timezone against IP location, TCP and TLS fingerprints, browser claims that disagree with the network. A VPN swaps the IP and leaves most of the rest standing, which lines up with the "suspicious signals" wording people quote from their ban emails. I expect the rules to be enforced properly, and the motive is on the record: the same announcement that closed the subsidiary route named distillation and adversarial governments outright, and geopolitics keeps that pressure on. As with most enforcement, the early targets are the low-hanging fruit, cheap signals before anything exotic. The gap between what gets banned and what could be is a precision/recall tradeoff Anthropic is still tuning, and personal accounts are where the cheap signals point first.
