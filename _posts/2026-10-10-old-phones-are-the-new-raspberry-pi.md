SWE-bench Verified is basically saturated now. [Vals archived the leaderboard in September](https://www.vals.ai/benchmarks/swebench) with seven models clustered between 95% and 97%, and DeepSeek V4 Pro solving issues for about ten cents each. Practically speaking, that means replicating how existing software behaves is getting very cheap. I think this is going to trigger a lot more rooting of consumer hardware. Previously, even if you unlocked a device, rebuilding the software stack or writing glue code took way too much effort for anyone outside hardcore hobbyists. Now an agent can just do most of that work for you.

Once software isn't the bottleneck, the hardware math looks pretty obvious. In the past, people bought Raspberry Pis to tinker or run small projects, but Pis aren't really cheap anymore once you add a case, power supply, SD card, monitor, and other controls. A used smartphone is a much better deal. Phones run on roughly an 18-month upgrade cycle and are heavily optimized for general use, so they get very cheap on the secondhand market (on [Swappa](https://swappa.com/blog/used-electronics-resale-data), a used Galaxy S22 goes for around $177, roughly the price of a bare [8GB Pi 5](https://www.canakit.com/raspberry-pi-5-8gb.html)). For that you get a better chip, a screen, battery, cameras, and connectivity already packed together. If rooting them and replacing the software becomes easy, I expect repurposed consumer hardware to become the default for a lot of small projects, and it should cut down on e-waste too.
